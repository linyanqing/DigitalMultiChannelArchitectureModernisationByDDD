Case Study – Digital Multi-Channel Architecture
(Fictional scenario: a payments and business-banking provider for small and medium merchants)


Session format

You will facilitate a collaborative architecture workshop with three stakeholders:
  - Principal Engineer
  - Engineering Lead
  - Product Manager

During the session you are expected to:
  - build a shared understanding of the current state
  - articulate the problems, goals and objectives
  - propose a pragmatic target architecture and an adoption approach
  - design and visualise the solution, considering:
      - short-term vs long-term outcomes
      - key risks and dependencies
      - business and technology constraints
  - explain the key design decisions and trade-offs

Logistics
  - 15 minutes of individual preparation to read the brief and note questions
  - 90 minutes of workshop with the stakeholders (whiteboard / paper available)
  - The facilitator keeps time and keeps the discussion focused on the goals


Problem context

The company's customer experience has evolved organically across several channels, each run by a
separate team. This has produced inefficient customer journeys, inconsistent experiences between
channels and slow feature delivery.

The company wants a more coherent digital experience across web and mobile for two merchant
journeys that are currently driven by paper forms:
  1. new customer onboarding
  2. in-life customer profile maintenance
It also wants faster delivery and more efficient maintenance and operation of its digital channels.

Business targets for the next two planning cycles:
  - convert 80% of existing paper forms into digital self-service experiences
  - increase speed of delivery by 30%
Feature delivery must continue while the architecture is modernised.


Current architecture and technology

  - Web channel: legacy server-side rendered application (JSP).
  - Mobile channels: native iOS (Swift) and native Android (Kotlin) apps.
  - Backend: Java/Spring microservices exposing domain APIs (e.g. Customer, Banking, Lending,
    Payments/Acquiring).
  - Each channel has its own orchestration / BFF-style service, however:
      - some channels bypass this layer and call domain APIs directly
      - some domain APIs are channel-specific (e.g. built for the iOS app)
      - business logic has leaked into the web/mobile apps and channel-specific layers
  - Staff-facing web applications exist per domain and are fragmented in tech stack and architecture.


Engineering and delivery practices

  - Web, iOS and Android teams use different stacks and patterns for build, release, testing and
    observability.
  - Engineers' skills are specialised to the channel they support.
  - Web releases more often than mobile, causing release coordination and feature-parity issues.


Additional constraints

  - Meaningful progress must be visible within two planning cycles (about 6 months).
  - Feature delivery cannot stop for a full rebuild, and rebuilding everything from scratch is
    not preferred.
  - The enterprise direction is domain-aligned platforms and reusable APIs.
  - Product wants consistent journeys but needs room for channel-specific differences where
    they add value.


Guidance

  - Ask clarifying questions; the brief intentionally leaves gaps.
  - Expect new information or constraints to be introduced during the session, which may
    invalidate an earlier approach.
  - Consider how a team would build, run and support the solution over the long term.
  - Keep API discussion at a high level (e.g. separate APIs for performing a change vs viewing
    history) rather than field-level detail.
  - Prioritise the reasoning and the journey over reaching an "ideal" answer; a well-reasoned
    discussion of one decision is worth more than many quick decisions.


Focus areas

  - translating digital strategy into architecture
  - web and mobile target architecture
  - cross-channel patterns
  - digital platform capabilities
  - paved-road thinking
  - sequencing and adoption
